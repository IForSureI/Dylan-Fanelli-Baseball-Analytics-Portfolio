## Summary

- **What he is:** a power right-hander who misses bats at an elite college rate with a 94-97 fastball and an 85-87 slider, behind walks and hit batters that are still a starter's problem. The question that decides his round is whether the fastball finds the zone often enough to start.
- **Role:** OFP 55, No. 3-4 starter. Fallback: high-leverage reliever, where the fastball and slider already play.
- **Starter call:** Needs Development. The named piece is fastball command, with a third pitch for left-handed hitters close behind.
- **Separator pitch:** the slider. Prep Baseball Report had it at a 56.6% whiff rate (its 99th percentile) and a 69.4% strike rate through Apr. 23, 2026.
- **Risk:** High. **ETA:** 2030 (my estimate for a 2027 college draftee who starts).
- **Round range:** 1st round, picks 10-20. **Investment:** full slot in that range, not over slot.
- **Recommendation:** Preference List. Next look: opening weekend, February 2027.

## Results

He pitched every one of his 15 starts on a weekend or in the postseason, none midweek: the second game of the weekend through March, then the series opener in six of his seven April and May starts, and the regional opener (starting-lineup table in Wake Forest's NCAA Tournament notes, May 28, 2026). Prep Baseball Report called him "Wake's ace" (Apr. 23, 2026). He missed bats from the first weekend to the last, with 14 strikeouts in six hitless innings against Loyola Marymount on Feb. 28 and 10 at Duke on May 14. He kept right-handed hitters almost helpless (.135) while lefties hit .244 off him. The damage he allowed came from free passes and the occasional fly ball: 33 walks, 7 hit batters and 7 home runs in 298 batters. In his biggest spot, the Morgantown Regional opener against Kentucky on May 29, he gave up one run in five innings with six strikeouts, but needed 96 pitches to get 15 outs.

## Base rates for the round range

- **Reaching the majors.** Of first-round college pitchers signed in 1996-2011, 162 of 226 (71.7%) reached the majors (Karcher, SABR Baseball Research Journal, 2017). First-rounders overall: 66.7% made it and 46.8% played in more than three seasons.
- **Starting.** Of four-year-college right-handers taken in the first round, 71.4% started at least one major league game (Cooper, Baseball America, Jul. 10, 2023). The article's 25-or-more-starts figure for the same group, 24.3%, comes from a table it doesn't label by round, so I treat it as approximate.
- **What the pick is worth.** On FanGraphs' 2019 values (Edwards, Apr. 5, 2019; 1993-2007 drafts, net of slot), pick 10 is worth $23.3M, pick 15 $18.4M and pick 20 $14.8M. My own draft study found that first-round pitchers return about two thirds of what first-round hitters do (65% over six seasons, 68% over the service window) ([draft analytics](../2026-mlb-draft-analytics.md)).
- **What this means for him:** a 71% chance he pitches in the majors at all is the base for a first-round college arm, and the bust risk for pitchers runs well above hitters'. His case to beat that base is the bat-missing; the walks are the case against.

## The round range and investment

- **1st round, picks 10-20.** MLB.com's way-too-early mock has him seventh (Jonathan Mayo, Jul. 14, 2026). I have him a few picks lower, because his value depends on command and a third pitch, neither proven, and his workload (4.9 innings a start) hasn't shown a starter's length.
- **Investment:** full slot in that range. He has already turned down second-round money once, and he has a senior year as leverage, so I don't see an under-slot path. I don't see an over-slot case either until the command question is answered.
- **What moves him up:** walks under about 9% across the first six weekends of 2027, a carry fastball on the readout, and a pitch that gets left-handers out. **What moves him down:** walks at 11%+ with a velocity dip past 75 pitches, which points to the bullpen.

## The open question for the next look

**Can he throw his fastball for strikes for five innings?** At the February 2027 opening weekend, with a gun and a TrackMan readout if the park has one:
1. Fastball strike rate and where the misses go (arm side, up, or spiked), inning by inning.
2. Fastball shape: induced vertical break, approach angle and extension. Is the 94-97 a carry fastball?
3. What he throws to left-handed hitters: cutter, curveball or changeup, and in what counts.
4. Velocity after pitch 60 and after pitch 80.
5. Delivery: effort, landing direction, and whether he repeats it from the stretch.
6. Body: the build and the face, to settle how much strength is still to come.
