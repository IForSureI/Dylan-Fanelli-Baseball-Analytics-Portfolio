# The Edge Board, 2026: a monthly league scan

**At a glance**

- **The question:** What changed across the league this month?
- **The answer:** Release tells, 292 candidate in-season pitch changes in 2026, results beyond stuff, and next-season whiff projections that cut error 6.4% on a held-out season against a regressed baseline.
- **For a club:** A list of leads for pro scouts and coaches. The toolkit rebuilds it monthly; this page shows the Oct. 17 run.

*Written 2026-10-17 by Dylan Fanelli. Data: Statcast via Baseball Savant, 2024-2026 regular seasons (2,141,219 pitches). Code: `baseball/edge/` (`bb edge 2026`, then `bb board 2026`).*

The toolkit rebuilds the board monthly. These are the numbers behind the README's summary. The page was written from the Oct. 17 run and has not had a monthly refresh since; on Nov. 14 every number here was reset to the saved run's table, which moved four of them in the last digit. Revised 2026-11-14: a held-out test of the projection, plainer labels on the pitch changes, and a shuffle test of how often the pitch-change detector fires by chance. Revised 2026-11-15: benchmarks of expected whiffs against Baseball Savant's public leaderboards and against FanGraphs Stuff+ (held out), a diagnosis of the model's weakness on sweepers, a second, location-aware measure tested for them, and a measure of refit noise. The numbers are saved in [`outputs/edge-2026.json`](outputs/edge-2026.json).

**Every pitch, in a table you can filter:** the [Edge Board dashboard](https://iforsurei.github.io/Dylan-Fanelli-Baseball-Analytics-Portfolio/edge-board.html) lists all 1,889 pitches of this run with the numbers below, per pitcher (`bb board-web 2026`).

## What it found
- **Candidate in-season pitch changes: 292 in 2026, by 225 pitchers.** A candidate counts when one pitch moved on its own (not the pitcher's whole arsenal), the label check passed (no relabel), and there were 40+ swings on each side of the date. One row per pitch, kept on its strongest signal: induced vertical break 109, arm-side break 95, velocity 59, spin 29. (The Oct. 17 page said 291 and 108; the saved table gives one more.)
  - **About nine in ten are more than chance order (measured Nov. 14).** The detector tries every split date and keeps the best one, with a high bar (|t| of 6 or more, and three of four later games on the new side). To see how often that fires when nothing changed, each pitcher's games were put in random order and the same detector and filter were rerun: 200 shuffled seasons, seed 20261114, 868 pitchers, 2,144 pitcher-pitch series. Pitches stay in their game and a pitcher's pitch types move together.
    - A shuffled season gave about 33 candidates on average (11% of the 292). The 200 runs ranged from 20 to 50.
    - So expect about 33 of the 292 to be chance. No row says which ones.
    - About 16 of the 33 land on pitches that are on the real list. A pitch with a real change can still split by chance once its games are shuffled, so 33 is likely high for pitchers who changed nothing.
    - Why chance gets through a bar of 6: the t counts every pitch as independent, and pitches in one game move together.
  - **They are still leads.** The shuffle removes everything tied to the calendar. It cannot tell a deliberate change from slow drift, a run of games in one park, or a tracking change. In 43 of the 292, a straight line through the season fits the game averages at least as well as the step does, and those read as drift. A scout or coach has to confirm each one. Saved in [`outputs/edge-shift-null-2026.json`](outputs/edge-shift-null-2026.json) (`python -m baseball.edge.shift_null 2026`).
  - **Did results follow?** Of the 292, 28 (9.6%) show a whiff change at p < 0.05, where chance alone gives about 5%. None clears a 5% false-discovery rate and 4 clear 10%. So most of these shape changes came with no detectable change in whiffs.
- **Results beyond stuff persist.** The gap between a pitch's actual and expected whiff rate carries over: r = 0.56 (2024 → 2025, 997 pitches) and 0.53 (2025 → 2026, 1,040).
- **Next-season whiff projection, on a season it never saw (added Nov. 14).** Weights fit on 2024 → 2025 (997 pitches) and scored on 2025 → 2026 (1,040 pitches): last season's whiff rate plus expected whiffs cut error 6.4% against a regressed last-season baseline (RMSE 5.20 vs. 5.56; 95% interval 4.9% to 8.0%, resampling pitchers). Raw last season scores 5.96. On pitches with 150 or fewer swings the year before (450 of them) it is 6.01 vs. 6.61 (`validate_holdout()` in `baseball/edge/stuff.py`).
- **The two-season version is leave-one-out, not held out.** Two seasons of results plus expected whiffs cut leave-one-out error 8.2% against a regressed last-season baseline (RMSE 4.62 vs. 5.03 on 659 pitches). Expected whiffs add 2.7 points of that. It needs three seasons per pitch, so 2026 is its only target year and no later season exists to score it on.
- **Outside benchmark: Baseball Savant's public leaderboards (added Nov. 15). This is not a Stuff+ comparison.** I matched my pitch tables for 2024-2026 to two Savant tables, on MLBAM pitcher ID and pitch type, from each page's own CSV download (pulled Nov. 15).
  - **The two tables.** Pitch Arsenal Stats gives whiff% and run value per 100 pitches. Swing Timing + Miss Distance, added in June 2026, gives miss distance: how far the barrel half of the bat was from the ball, in inches, averaged over swings and misses. Miss distance is a result from games, like whiff rate. It is the closest thing Savant publishes to a stuff measure, and it is not a physics model.
  - **Matching.** Statcast pitch codes on both sides. Savant files knuckle curves under curveball, so my KC rows match its CU row. Floors: 60 swings on my side in each season; 50 pitches (Arsenal Stats) and 50 swings (Miss Distance) in Savant's files. Intervals are 95%, resampling pitchers.
  - **The pipeline matches.** My whiff rate from the pitch files sits 0.03 to 0.04 points from Savant's published whiff% on average in each season. In 2026 that is 0.04 on 1,698 pitches, and 98% equal the one decimal Savant prints. Savant counts foul tips as whiffs, as I do. Without them the gap would be about 2.1 points. The model's tables use only swings with complete tracking and sit 0.10 to 0.14 away.
  - **Same season, expected whiff tracks miss distance mostly through pitch type.** All pitches together: r = 0.71 (0.70 to 0.73) on 4,964 pitches by 888 pitchers. Each pitch against others of its type: r = 0.25 (0.21 to 0.29).
  - **It barely tracks run value.** Against Savant's run value per 100: r = 0.04 (0.01 to 0.07) together and 0.11 (0.08 to 0.14) within type, on 4,973 pitches. My actual whiff rate reaches 0.20 and 0.30. Expected whiff is a read on misses, not on run prevention.
  - **Next season's whiff rate: last season's whiff rate wins alone.** Weights fit on 2024 → 2025 (994 pitches) and scored on 2025 → 2026 (1,040 pitches, 395 pitchers), every model on the same pitches. RMSE: last season's whiff rate 5.57, my expected whiff 6.37, Savant's miss distance 6.97.
  - **Mine beats raw miss distance and ties it once pitch type is known.** Raw, mine is ahead by 0.61 (0.28 to 0.92). But miss distance does not know what pitch it is looking at: the average four-seamer here misses by 1.0 inches and the average curveball by 4.6. Given the pitch type, miss distance scores 6.03 and mine 6.18: a tie (difference 0.15, interval -0.07 to 0.39) with miss distance nominally ahead.
  - **Added to last season's whiff rate, the same picture.** Mine cuts error 6.5% (5.0% to 8.1%) against the regressed baseline. Miss distance cuts 4.2% (2.6% to 5.8%). Given the pitch type they tie at 7.2% and 7.3%. (The 6.4% above is the same test with three more pitches in the fit.)
  - **Together is best.** All three cut 7.6% (5.8% to 9.5%). Miss distance takes 0.06 (0.01 to 0.12) of RMSE off my blend, so it holds something my model lacks.
  - **Fit the other way** (2025 → 2026 weights scored on 2024 → 2025, 994 pitches): alone, 5.62, 6.83 and 7.25 in the same order. With last season's whiff rate the two tie (4.1% and 3.3%), and miss distance again adds 0.07 (0.03 to 0.11).
  - **The verdict.** My expected whiff predicts next season about as well as Savant's miss distance read within pitch type, and no better. Neither beats last season's whiff rate alone. What mine has that miss distance lacks: it needs no swings, so it can grade a bullpen session or a TrackMan pitch.
  - **Refit check.** A second fit of the model from the same pitch files moves expected whiff 0.5 points per pitch on average (r = 0.996 between fits). The verdicts are the same and the gain is 6.5%.
  - Saved in [`outputs/edge-savant-benchmark-2026.json`](outputs/edge-savant-benchmark-2026.json) (`python -m baseball.edge.savant_benchmark 2026`). The downloaded Savant files stay out of the repository.
- **Stuff+ benchmark (added Nov. 15): FanGraphs Stuff+ by pitch type, 2024 to 2026.** Stuff+ beats my expected whiff inside a pitch type. Once last season's whiff rate is in the model, the two tie, and each adds a little to the other.
  - **The tables.** The FanGraphs Major League pitching leaderboard, Stuff+ tab, single seasons, as shown on the public page on Nov. 15, 2026. They hold 588 of 855 pitchers for 2024 (everyone with 15+ innings), 734 of 873 for 2025 (the 139 missing all threw under 15 innings) and 597 of 868 for 2026 (everyone with 15+ innings). The tables stay out of the repository.
  - **Different targets.** Stuff+ is built to predict run value. My expected whiff is built to predict swings and misses. Read every line below with that in mind.
  - **IDs.** FanGraphs ID to MLBAM ID through the Chadwick register: 588 of 588 rows for 2024, 734 of 734 for 2025 and 514 of 597 for 2026. The 83 without a link are left out. I matched no one by name.
  - **Pitch types: one rule.** FF to FA. SI, FC, FS, FO, CH and KC to the column of the same name. Sweepers go to FanGraphs' SL column and slurves to its CU column. A pitcher who throws both pitches of a shared column has one Stuff+ number for the two, so my pitch is matched only when it is at least 90% of his pitches in that column. That drops 106 pitches in 2024, 110 in 2025 and 125 in 2026.
  - **Why that rule.** Pitchers with 100+ sweepers and no slider have a value in the SL column 94 times of 95 (2025) and 79 of 79 (2026). Pitchers with no slider, sweeper or slurve have one 6 times of 54 and 0 of 27. Pitchers with 100+ slurves and no slider or sweeper: SL filled 0 of 6 and 0 of 5, CU filled 6 of 6 and 5 of 5. That slurve count is small, and 2024 is less clean (SL filled 3 of 5, CU 4 of 5). Slurves are 3, 6 and 4 of the matched pitches in the three seasons, so the choice moves nothing. Knuckle curves sit in KC: 33 of 36 and 28 of 29.
  - **Same season, my expected whiff against Stuff+ (2025 and 2026).** r = 0.35 (0.31 to 0.39) across all pitches and 0.47 (0.43 to 0.52) within pitch type, on 2,948 pitches by 659 pitchers (60+ swings, intervals resample pitchers). By type it runs 0.38 to 0.61, except sweepers: r = 0.00 (-0.16 to 0.14) on 174 sweepers.
  - **Three measures side by side, same pitches, within pitch type.** Against the season's own whiff rate: Stuff+ 0.46, Savant miss distance 0.41, my expected whiff 0.36. Against Savant's run value per 100: Stuff+ 0.26 (0.22 to 0.30), mine 0.11 (0.07 to 0.15), miss distance 0.10 (0.06 to 0.14). Stuff+ has the higher number on its own target and on mine.
  - **Pitcher level.** My swing-weighted expected whiff against overall Stuff+: r = 0.29 (0.21 to 0.36) on 1,059 pitcher-seasons (662 pitchers). Against run value per 100, Stuff+ is 0.31 (0.25 to 0.36) and mine 0.17 (0.10 to 0.23).
  - **Next season's whiff rate, held out.** Every model's weights are fit on 2024 → 2025 (918 pitches) and scored on 2025 → 2026 (963 pitches, 388 pitchers), which no weight has seen. Pitchers have 15+ innings in the earlier season of each pair.
  - **Why the innings floor.** The 2024 and 2026 tables stop at 15 innings. The 2025 table keeps shorter pitchers only from its first 708 rows by Pitching+, so those lean toward good ones. It barely matters here: 3 of the 966 scored pitches come from such pitchers, and with them left in the Stuff+ lead below is 0.21 (0.05 to 0.36).
  - **Alone, last season's whiff rate still wins** (RMSE 5.58). Raw Stuff+ scores 8.98 because it is scaled inside each pitch type and carries no pitch-type level. The fair read gives each measure the pitch type.
  - **Given the pitch type, Stuff+ beats my model.** Stuff+ 5.84, miss distance 5.94, mine 6.05. Stuff+ is ahead of mine by 0.21 (0.05 to 0.37). Stuff+ and miss distance tie (difference -0.10, interval -0.32 to 0.12).
  - **Added to last season's whiff rate and the pitch type, all three tie.** Cut in error against the regressed baseline: mine 7.8% (6.0% to 9.5%), Stuff+ 7.7% (5.7% to 9.5%), miss distance 7.6% (5.7% to 9.4%). Mine against Stuff+: difference -0.01, interval -0.05 to 0.04.
  - **Each adds a little to the other.** Adding Stuff+ to my blend with pitch type takes 0.04 (0.01 to 0.07) off RMSE. Adding my expected whiff to the Stuff+ blend takes 0.04 (0.02 to 0.07) off. All three measures with last season and pitch type cut 9.6% (7.7% to 11.5%).
  - **Cross-validated, for comparison.** Before the 2024 table existed I scored this inside 2025 → 2026 by five-fold cross-validation by pitcher (966 pitches). It gave the same answers: Stuff+ ahead of mine by 0.21 (0.03 to 0.39) given the pitch type, a tie once last season is in (difference 0.01, interval -0.07 to 0.08). One thing changed: the gain from adding Stuff+ to my blend was not clear there (-0.04, interval -0.09 to 0.01) and is clear held out.
  - **Limits.** One held-out test, and no second scoring year until 2027. Stuff+ by pitch type only for the types FanGraphs lists (FA, SI, FC, FS, SL, CU, CH, KC, FO). The 2025 table is missing 139 pitchers under 15 innings. The 2026 table has 83 pitchers the register does not link yet.
  - Saved in [`outputs/edge-stuffplus-benchmark-2026.json`](outputs/edge-stuffplus-benchmark-2026.json) (`python -m baseball.edge.stuffplus_benchmark 2026`).
- **Sweepers: the stuff model is weak there, the cause is known, and no physics-only fix worked (Nov. 15).** The stuff model is unchanged, so no published number moved. The next bullet adds a second, location-aware measure.
  - **How weak.** Across pitchers with 60+ swings, expected against actual whiff rate on sweepers: r = 0.23, 0.14 and 0.14 in 2024, 2025 and 2026 (139, 141 and 151 sweepers). On sliders: 0.31, 0.43 and 0.41.
  - **It is not a sign or labeling error.** The average is right: 31.3% expected against 32.2% actual on 2025 sweepers. Swing by swing the model sorts sweepers as well as sliders (AUC inside the type 0.56 and 0.56 in 2025). What it misses is which pitcher's sweeper is better.
  - **Why: sweeper whiffs follow how the pitch is used, not its shape.** Across 431 sweeper seasons, the share of swings that are chases tracks the whiff rate at r = 0.62 (0.54 to 0.69). The model leaves location out on purpose. Of the physical measures the best is the pitcher's fastball velocity (which the model can work out from two of its inputs), r = 0.28 (0.15 to 0.41), and no other passes 0.16. Sliders have shape signal to find: speed gap to the fastball -0.36, induced vertical break -0.23.
  - **What the model leans on instead.** Among sweepers its expected whiff follows velocity (0.57) and extension (0.38). Those track sweeper whiffs at 0.16 and 0.03.
  - **Hitter side.** Against same-side hitters r = 0.15, 0.03 and 0.14; against opposite-side hitters 0.34, 0.33 and 0.29.
  - **The signal exists; my inputs do not hold it.** A sweeper's actual whiff rate carries over (r = 0.51 and 0.65), but last season's expected whiff sees next season's actual at 0.04 and 0.14. A model fit on sweepers alone (74,649 swings, three seasons) reaches r = 0.21 on 431 sweeper seasons.
  - **Fixes tried, with the rule set first.** A change is adopted only if it lowers held-out error on sweepers (weights fit on 2024 → 2025, scored on 2025 → 2026, interval excludes zero) and makes no pitch type clearly worse. Change in sweeper RMSE on 91 sweepers: pitch type as an input -0.01 (-0.06 to 0.04); the fastball's velocity as an input +0.08 (-0.03 to 0.22); its velocity and shape +0.11 (-0.04 to 0.29). None passes. None is clearly worse elsewhere either. I did not tune anything to Stuff+.
  - **What to do with it.** On a sweeper, read the actual whiff rate and treat my physics-only expected whiff as the average for the type. No sweeper grade from this model appears in any report here (checked Nov. 15). The [pitch-design study](2026-pitch-design-levers.md) had already found that nothing in it predicts sweeper whiff changes.
  - Saved in [`outputs/edge-stuff-checks-2026.json`](outputs/edge-stuff-checks-2026.json) (`python -m baseball.edge.stuff_checks 2026`).
- **A second measure for sweepers: location-aware expected whiff (added Nov. 15).** It passes the held-out test as a read on a sweeper in place of my physics number. It does not beat simply reading last season's whiff rate, and it does not go into the projection.
  - **What it is.** The same model, fit the same way (cross-fit by pitcher), with four more inputs for each swing: where the pitch crossed the plate (sideways toward or away from the hitter, and height as a share of his strike zone) and the balls and strikes. A pitch's number is the average over the swings it drew.
  - **What it is not.** It is not stuff. Location is partly the pitcher's command, partly the catcher and the plan, and partly which pitches hitters chose to swing at. The stuff grade keeps its meaning (physics only) and its name. This one is called location-aware expected whiff everywhere.
  - **No leakage.** In the same season a pitcher's number comes from a model that never saw his swings. Predicting next season, every input is the earlier season's.
  - **Same season, sweepers.** Against the actual whiff rate: r = 0.75, 0.71 and 0.71 in 2024, 2025 and 2026, where the physics number gave 0.23, 0.14 and 0.14. On the 174 sweepers matched to Stuff+ it is 0.67 (0.53 to 0.77); Stuff+ was 0.44 there. It is higher on every pitch type, which is expected of a number that sees where swings happened.
  - **Next season, the test that counts.** Weights fit on 2024 → 2025 (997 pitches), scored on 2025 → 2026 (1,040 pitches). Last season's location-aware number sees next season's sweeper whiff rate at r = 0.61; the physics number at 0.14; last season's whiff rate itself at 0.65.
  - **Used alone, it beats the physics number.** Change in held-out RMSE: all pitches -0.65 (-0.87 to -0.44); sweepers -1.71 (-2.54 to -0.82) on 91. The interval is off zero for changeups, curveballs, sinkers and sweepers, and no pitch type is clearly worse. By the rule set before scoring, it is adopted as the model read for those types.
  - **Added to last season's whiff rate, it is no better than the physics number.** Change against the published blend: all pitches +0.04 (-0.02 to 0.10), sweepers -0.02 (-0.28 to 0.24), and sliders are clearly worse (+0.24 (0.03 to 0.44)). So the projection keeps the physics number. Putting both in moves all pitches -0.03 (-0.07 to 0.00).
  - **And it does not beat last season's whiff rate.** Alone it scores 5.70 against 5.56 for last season's whiff rate. Added to that rate it changes sweeper RMSE by -0.27 (-0.61 to 0.07), which is not clear.
  - **The other direction** (weights from 2025 → 2026, scored on 2024 → 2025, 997 pitches) agrees on the whole and is weaker on sweepers: all pitches -0.81 (-1.04 to -0.57) alone, sweepers -0.81 (-1.60 to 0.05) on 76, next-season r 0.32 against 0.04.
  - **Beside Stuff+ (extra rows in the Stuff+ benchmark, 963 pitches, same held-out design).** Given the pitch type, location-aware scores 5.55 against 5.84 for Stuff+ and 6.05 for my physics number; it is ahead of Stuff+ by 0.29 (0.07 to 0.51). That is not a like-for-like win: Stuff+ does not see location. With last season's whiff rate and pitch type they tie (difference 0.01, interval -0.05 to 0.06).
  - **How to use it.** On a sweeper with a season of swings, read the actual whiff rate first. Where a model number is wanted, use the location-aware one and say what it includes. My stuff grade on a sweeper stays unproven.
  - Saved in [`outputs/edge-stuff-location-2026.json`](outputs/edge-stuff-location-2026.json) (`python -m baseball.edge.stuff_location 2026`).
- **Refit noise, measured (Nov. 15).** Fitting the model again does not give the same numbers to the last digit. I measured how much they move.
  - **Row order alone does it.** The cross-fit folds and the model's binning both depend on the order of the swings. The same 2025 swings fit in three other orders move a pitch's expected whiff by 0.64 to 0.65 points on average (r = 0.992 or better with the first fit). The largest move on one pitch is 6.5 to 8.2 points.
  - **It is not chance in the fit.** The seed is fixed, and two fits of the same rows in the same order on one machine agree exactly.
  - **What it explains.** My refit from the same pitch files sat 0.5 points per pitch from the saved tables and gave a held-out gain of 6.5% against the published 6.4%. That gap is smaller than a reordering makes. I cannot recover the row order or the library versions of the saved run (the lock file pins numpy 2.4.6 and my refit ran on a newer one), so I can say the gap is the size these produce, not which one it was.
  - **What it means for a grade.** One pitch's expected whiff can move several points between fits. It is one more reason a grade is a band. Averaging several cross-fits would shrink it. That is not done. For the ten pitches the [bullpen memo](2026-bullpen-acquisition-memo.md) grades, ten refits kept 19 of the 20 stuff and 2027 grades within 5 points of the printed one; the exception is Fairbanks's slider stuff grade, 35 to 50 ([`outputs/edge-grade-noise-2026.json`](outputs/edge-grade-noise-2026.json)).
- **What the grades built on it mean:** the 20-80 pitch grades in the bullpen memo and the Morejón report come from this model. With a held-out gain of about 6%, a grade is a half-grade band, not a point.
- **Release tells and whiffs.** Pooled 2024-26 with errors clustered by pitcher: +0.45 whiff points per 0.1 of tell AUC (95% interval +0.09 to +0.82). With usage and context controlled it falls to +0.19 (-0.12 to +0.50), so no cost is detected.
- **The stuff model** reads swings and misses from physics alone with a cross-fitted AUC of 0.633; the portable version for TrackMan and Rapsodo data, without arm angle, is 0.631.

## League baselines
Every season-over-season change is read against the league's: for each pitch type, the distribution of every returning pitcher's change. The Skubal report uses it to settle whether his 2025 jump was him or the cameras ([report](2025-skubal-arsenal.md)): his changes sat at the 80th-98th percentile while the league median barely moved.
